Lilac lets you choose which AI model powers each task you run. Different models have different strengths — some are faster and lighter, making them ideal for quick edits and simple requests, while others are more capable and thorough, suited to complex reasoning and large projects. Picking the right model helps you get better results faster and makes the most efficient use of your resources.

## Where to Select a Model

The model selector is available in the workspace before you start a task. Look for the model name displayed near the task input area — click the name or the selector dropdown to see all available options. Switch between models at any time by selecting a different one from the list. Your selection applies to the next task you run.

## Choosing the Right Model

Different categories of models suit different kinds of work. Use the guide below to match the model to your task.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Fast & Efficient" icon="bolt" href="/account/plan-limits">
    Best for simple tasks, quick edits, and first drafts. Responds faster and costs less, making it a good fit for rapid iterations and small changes where deep reasoning isn't required.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Balanced" icon="scale-balanced" href="/account/plan-limits">
    A reliable default for most tasks — coding, writing, and research. Offers a strong mix of speed and capability, so it handles the majority of real-world work well without the overhead of the most powerful models.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Most Capable" icon="star" href="/account/plan-limits">
    Best for complex, multi-step work that requires deep reasoning, handling large codebases, or producing the most thorough and accurate results. Takes longer to respond but delivers the highest quality output for demanding tasks.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Tips for Model Selection

<Tip>
  **Start with the Balanced model for most tasks.** It handles a wide range of work reliably and is a sensible default until you have a reason to switch.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  **Switch to Most Capable if the agent struggles.** If the agent gives incomplete answers, misses steps in a complex task, or produces results that need heavy correction, switching to the Most Capable model often resolves the issue.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  **Use Fast & Efficient for quick iterations.** When you're reviewing small changes, testing a prompt, or making minor adjustments, the faster model keeps the feedback loop tight without sacrificing much quality.
</Tip>

<Note>
  The models available to you depend on your current plan. See the [plan limits](/account/plan-limits) page for a full breakdown of which models are included at each tier.
</Note>
